Most founders try YouTube, upload ten videos, get 200 views each, and quit. They conclude YouTube doesn't work for their business.

YouTube worked fine. They just built the wrong machine. They optimized for viewers. Viewers don't pay invoices. Buyers do.

This is the playbook we run at GenSpeak to turn B2B channels into client pipelines. It works at small subscriber counts. It works in boring niches. It works precisely because it ignores most creator advice.

The mistake: one engine instead of two

A channel that produces clients needs two engines running at once:

  • A growth engine. Gets the right people to watch. Packaging, topics with proven demand, formats that hold attention.
  • A conversion engine. Turns those people into leads and calls. Buyer-focused videos, lead magnets, email, a booking page.

Most businesses build only the first and wonder where the money is. Educator Ed Lawrence, who runs a mid six-figure education business off roughly 10,000 views a video, puts it bluntly: if you optimize for viewers you get viewers. If you optimize for buyers you make money. One of his worst-performing videos by views was one of his top earners.

That is the whole shift. Judge videos by calls booked, not views.

Step 1: Pick the one problem you want to own

Channels that cover five topics die. The person who found you through a pricing video won't watch your hiring video. Pick one specific, in-demand problem your ideal buyer knows they have, and make every video about it.

Write your goal in one sentence before anything else:

YouTube should help [your company] attract [specific buyer] so they can [business outcome].

If you can't fill that in, no amount of production quality will save the channel.

Step 2: Make buyer videos, not view videos

Four video types consistently produce sales calls for B2B channels:

  • Case study. The story of how you got a client the exact result your buyers want. Highest intent of anything you can publish.
  • Deep dive. A 20-minute-plus walkthrough of your actual framework with real steps. Proves you're an operator, not a blog.
  • Roast or teardown. Review a real business's setup on camera. It's a free taste of working with you.
  • Short process video. One specific outcome, five steps, no filler.

Listicles, news reactions, and trend commentary get views. They rarely get calls. Use them sparingly, and only to feed the growth engine.

Step 3: Title for the buyer's awareness level

Two kinds of titles, two jobs:

  • Problem-aware titles name the pain or the desired result. More people recognize themselves, so you get more views.
  • Solution-aware titles promise a specific fix. Only people who already know they need it will click. Fewer views, far more sales.

A healthy channel runs both. If your pipeline is empty, weight toward solution-aware.

Step 4: Open with a problem only buyers recognize

Generic problems repel buyers because buyers are past them. Open with the exact, specific thing your buyer worries about at 11pm. Casual viewers will leave. Let them. The retention graph dips and the pipeline fills.

Step 5: Give the right amount, then stop selling

Don't dump everything you know. The reaction you want is "this is simpler than I thought, I can do this." An inspired viewer books a call. An overwhelmed one closes the tab.

And skip the pitch. If the video did its job, a one-line CTA and a link is all you need. Switching into sales voice at the end breaks the trust the previous ten minutes built.

Step 6: Build the path from video to call

A video without a next step is a dead end. Every video should route somewhere:

  1. A lead magnet that extends the video: a template, a checklist, a tool.
  2. An opt-in page that trades it for an email.
  3. A short email sequence that deepens trust and offers a call.
  4. A booking page with zero friction.

What to measure

Track buyer videos on business metrics only: link clicks, emails captured, calls booked, clients signed. Track view videos on views. Never mix the scorecards. A case study video with 400 views and two booked calls is a massive win.
