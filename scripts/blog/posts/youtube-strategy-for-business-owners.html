Most business YouTube strategies are built backwards. They start with "what videos should we make?" That question comes fifth, not first.

This is the order we use to build every GenSpeak client strategy:

Business goal → buyer psychology → demand proof → proven formats → 12-week plan.

Skip a step and you get a channel that entertains strangers instead of booking calls.

Step 1: One sentence, or no channel

Before any research, write this sentence and make everyone agree to it:

YouTube should help [company] attract [specific audience] so they can [business outcome].

"Build authority" is not an outcome. "Book qualified sales calls for our fractional CFO service" is. A viral video that attracts the wrong audience is a loss dressed as a win, and without this sentence you can't even tell.

Step 2: Map the buyer, not the demographic

"Men, 35 to 55, US" tells you nothing about what to film. Map psychology instead:

  • What do they want? What are they afraid of?
  • What have they already tried that failed?
  • What language do they actually use for the problem?
  • What would make them trust you? What would make them buy?

Then pick one segment. A firm that serves both enterprises and solo operators cannot speak to both in one channel. Choose the segment that best supports the offer and write down which segments you are deliberately ignoring, so nobody relitigates it in month two.

Step 3: Tag every idea by awareness stage

Every video idea gets tagged with where the viewer is:

  • Problem aware: feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist. Broad topics, top of funnel.
  • Solution aware: knows the type of fix, comparing approaches. Tactical mechanism videos, middle of funnel.
  • Most aware: knows you, needs proof and a reason to act. Case studies, objection videos, comparisons. Bottom of funnel.

The simple rule: market-shift videos are top of funnel, mechanism videos are middle, proof videos are bottom. A channel that only makes one layer either gets views with no calls, or preaches to an audience of nobody.

Step 4: Prove demand before you film

Never film on a hunch. Every topic needs evidence that real people already care: outlier videos in your niche, repeated questions in comments, autocomplete suggestions, objections from sales calls. The full process is in video ideas for business.

The bar we use: a topic makes the plan when we can point at the source and say why it proves demand. "I think this would be interesting" doesn't clear the bar. For formats, we look for videos that beat their own channel's average by 3x or more, recently, with a structure we can remix.

Step 5: The 12-week plan

Twelve weeks, one video a week, is enough to prove or disprove a channel direction. Shape:

  • Mix the funnel. Roughly one bottom-of-funnel buyer video for every two or three reach videos at the start. Tilt further toward buyer videos as the channel matures.
  • Every video has a named job. Reach, trust, or calls. If you can't name the job, cut the video.
  • Every video has a next step. Lead magnet, related video, or booking link. Dead-end videos waste the click.

Review at week 12 against the goal sentence from step 1, not against subscriber count.

Positioning: own one word

The channels that convert are the ones where a buyer can finish this sentence: "that's the [X] person." One problem, one audience, relentlessly. Mixed-topic channels confuse the algorithm and the buyer equally. If you feel the urge to "branch out" in week six, that's usually impatience wearing a strategy costume.
