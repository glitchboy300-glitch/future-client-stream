A business video lives or dies in the first 8 seconds. New viewers give you that long before deciding to stay or leave, and the window is shrinking.

Here is how we script videos for GenSpeak clients: founders who are experts at their work, not performers. The system makes them watchable without making them sound like someone else.

Before you write: the script has one job

The title and thumbnail made a promise. The script's only job is to keep that promise, fast, and hold one open question in the viewer's head until the end. Everything that doesn't serve that gets cut.

The hook: confirm the click

The first sentence must make the topic unmistakably clear and prove the viewer clicked the right video. Rules:

  • Name or demonstrate the problem, promise, or tension immediately.
  • No biography. No "hey guys, welcome back." No scene-setting.
  • Open a genuine question the body will answer.
  • If you have proof, show it early. Numbers beat adjectives.

Strong hook lines lead with one of six elements: the subject, an action, an objective, a contrast, proof, or time. "We booked 47 sales calls from one video that took 2 hours to film" carries proof, time, and an objective in one sentence.

The 3-part intro

After the hook, run this sequence in under a minute:

  1. Click confirm. Restate the promise so the viewer relaxes: yes, this video is about the thing you clicked for.
  2. Common belief, then your take. Name what most people in your niche believe, then state where you disagree and why it matters.
  3. Proof and plan. One line of credibility, one line of roadmap. Then go.

The body: second-best first

Order your points deliberately: open with your second-strongest point, close with your strongest. Starting weak loses viewers early. Spending your best point first makes the back half sag.

Inside each point, run a small loop: open a question, build a little anticipation, close with a payoff the viewer didn't see coming, then open the next question. Chain those loops and retention takes care of itself.

Two craft notes:

  • Vary sentence length. Long explanatory sentences, then a short one. Like this. Monotone rhythm reads as boring even when the content is good.
  • Rehook every few minutes. Remind the viewer what's still coming: "and the third mistake is the one that costs the most."

Use proof honestly

When you have a verified result that relates to the topic, use it, and keep it precise: the real timeframe, the real context, the real limits. "One client booked 12 calls in his first 60 days" is believable. "This gets everyone insane results" is noise. Precision is what separates an operator from a guru, and buyers can smell the difference.

The ending: high note, no pitch

End on your strongest insight, not on a fade-out summary. Then one native CTA in your normal speaking voice: what to do next and why it helps them. If the video did its job, buyers sell themselves. An abrupt tone-shift into salesman voice undoes ten minutes of trust in ten seconds.

The read-aloud test

A script is finished when you can read it out loud and it sounds like you on a good day. If you stumble on a sentence, the sentence is wrong, not you. If a paragraph sounds like a copywriter wrote it, cut it and say what you'd actually say.

Founders shouldn't wing it on camera, and they shouldn't perform someone else's words either. The script is scaffolding for your judgment, not a costume.
